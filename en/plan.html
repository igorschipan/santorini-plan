Goal

The guest orders and pays in the app without waiting for a waiter, and the restaurant does not lose money. We get there in stages, adding at each stage whatever is already available.

The end result:

  • the guest scans a QR code and orders, the order goes straight into the POS and the kitchen, and the food is brought to the table;
  • the guest pays in the app, for each order or at the end on an open tab;
  • waiters bring food, help guests and handle unusual cases; the call button stays for guests who want a person;
  • the menu, prices and sold-out items come from the POS.

Each stage is switched on separately and can return to the previous one with one button. We propose when to move on, the owner approves.

Stage 4 without Probus

The waiter re-enters the app order in Order Taker by hand, and a mistake is possible here: the guest paid for one thing and the kitchen got another. So the waiter marks every order "transferred", and in the evening app orders are matched against the bills. At this stage menu prices must match PRESTO including tax and service charge, otherwise the guest pays the wrong amount.

How guests cannot leave without paying

One rule: an order from the app goes to the kitchen only if it is paid or covered by a hold on the guest's card. There are no unpaid orders from the app. Large QR ordering systems such as Toast in the USA work the same way: the guest chooses between paying per order and an open tab with a card on file.

Pay per order (from stage 4)

The guest pays when sending the order, by QRIS, card or e-wallet. An unpaid order is not cooked, so nobody can leave without paying. This works with QRIS too, the main local payment method.

Open tab on a card (stage 5)

  • On the first order the guest's bank holds an amount on the card, for example Rp 1,000,000. No money is taken yet.
  • The guest keeps ordering while the bill stays below the hold. When the bill nears the limit, the next order needs a top-up or a new hold.
  • At the end the guest taps "Close the bill": the exact amount is charged and the rest is released.
  • If the guest leaves without closing, the bill closes by itself after a set time without orders or at closing time, and the amount is charged from the hold.

This mode needs the Probus connection: to close a bill ourselves we need the exact bill total from the POS, including whatever the waiter added.

Limits:

  • Midtrans and Xendit support holds on cards only. QRIS does not, so a guest paying by QRIS can only pay per order.
  • A hold lasts about 7 days, then the bank releases it. So every bill is closed the same evening.
  • The charge cannot exceed the hold.
  • A hold is not a guarantee: the guest's bank may refuse the charge. The risk is small but real.
  • At Xendit a hold can be charged only once. If the bill is split, the other guests pay their share separately and the tab owner pays the rest.
  • On a debit card the held amount reduces the available balance right away. The guest sees a warning on screen.

Items added by the waiter

Items the waiter adds in Order Taker go on the same table bill. On an open tab they are covered by the hold. With pay per order the waiter asks the guest to pay for them before leaving, as today. The bar screen and the tablets highlight tables with an unpaid amount not covered by a hold.

The Probus POS

  • Probus System is made by CV Mitra Solusi Mandiri in Denpasar, in business since 2010, with more than 250 clients, 75 of them hotels in Bali and Nusa Tenggara (Gili Air is in Nusa Tenggara too).
  • For restaurants: the PRESTO POS (floor plan, bill splitting, kitchen printing), the Order Taker app for waiters, a self-service kiosk where the guest orders and pays, stock and accounting.
  • Order Taker runs on Android, paired with PRESTO: floor plan, taking orders, several payment methods, bill splitting. According to AppBrain it was removed from Google Play on 3 December 2024; Probus apparently installs it on tablets itself now. Santorini's waiters already work on tablets.
  • PRESTO runs on a server inside the restaurant and works without internet. A backup is made every day.
  • The same company also sells Barong POS, a separate Android POS at Rp 250,000 a month that works differently from PRESTO. So the first thing to check is which POS the restaurant has; the plan below is written for PRESTO with Order Taker.
  • There is no public developer API, and the help site only describes modules. The website lists integrations with the STAAH and eZee channel managers, door locks, accounting and unnamed "payment gateways". Probus officially takes on custom work for clients.

Conclusion: we cannot connect to the POS on our own, an agreement with Probus is needed. Their kiosk already sends orders and payments into the POS from outside, so the POS most likely supports this.

How others do it

  • The menu service and the POS are linked by a bridge: an order from the phone enters the POS as an ordinary table order and goes to the kitchen as usual.
  • If the POS only works inside the restaurant, a small program or tablet is placed on site. It reaches out to the menu service and fetches orders itself. The POS is never opened to the internet.
  • Payment: the guest opens the table bill, the amount comes from the POS, and the POS closes the bill only after confirmation from the payment provider. A "paid" message on the guest's screen means nothing.
  • Calling a waiter does not need the POS at all: a button, a notification to the waiter, "Accept", and the call closes by itself after 10 minutes.

Two ways to connect

Option A, without Probus. The waiter opens the table bill in Order Taker (or the cashier in PRESTO) and enters the bill number and amount in our app, the guest pays, and the payment is then marked in the POS as a separate method, "Online QR". Quick to launch, but the amount is entered by hand. Paying straight from the menu without a waiter is switched on after the pilot.

Option B, with an upgrade from Probus. Our app reads the open table bill (items, tax, service charge, amount due) and marks the payment by itself. Order Taker gets a "Pay online" button: a QR code for the exact bill total, no manual entry. Waiter calls can be shown there too. Orders from the menu go straight into the POS and the kitchen. The connection is outgoing only: a small program next to the POS contacts our server, and the Probus server is never opened to the internet.

Calling a waiter

Buttons in the menu: "Call a waiter", "Bill: cash, card or QRIS", "Water". Each table has its own QR code with a long random key, and the general menu link cannot send calls.

Protection against pranks: one call of each type per table every minute or two, a cap on the number of calls, any table can be switched off with one button, and new QR codes are printed if one leaks.

Calls arrive in our app on the same tablets that run Order Taker and pop up over it. The tablets belong to the restaurant and are set up once, so nothing depends on personal phones. Best of all, Probus could show the calls inside Order Taker, so the waiter has a single app.

This needs a small server of ours, because the menu page is static today. The same Cloudflare account that hosts the menu will do. The tablets need internet; the restaurant network alone is not enough.

Why not Telegram, and why not only WhatsApp

In Bali people use WhatsApp, so Telegram is out. Everyone has WhatsApp and phones do not put it to sleep, but calls get lost among personal chats, and the volume and repeat of the signal cannot be set. It needs a separate restaurant number approved by Meta, and messages cost money: about Rp 360 per message to an Indonesian number under Meta's rates from 1 October 2026 (some messages are free if the person wrote to the bot in the last 24 hours). An "Accept" button inside the message is possible. So WhatsApp stays as a backup channel for the manager.

What can go wrong

  • The tablet lies on the counter while the waiter carries food: the sound does not reach everywhere and vibration cannot be felt.
  • Several waiters share one tablet, so it is unclear who took the call.
  • Order Taker works over the restaurant network, but our app needs internet, which can be unstable on Gili.
  • Tablets of some brands (Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Realme) put apps to sleep to save battery. An app swiped away from the recent list gets no notifications until it is opened. On Android 14 and later, full-screen calls like an alarm clock must be allowed by hand.
  • If the tablets turn out to be iPads: in silent mode there is only vibration. Apple lets apps override silent mode only with special approval, mostly for public services, healthcare and smart homes.

No single device guarantees delivery; a system of five layers does.

Five layers of protection

  1. The app on the tablet: its own loud signal, a full-screen call over Order Taker and an "Accept" button. When opened, the app fetches all open calls itself, even if a notification was lost.
  2. The signal repeats until someone taps "Accept". Each waiter signs in with a PIN at the start of the shift, so it is clear who accepted. Once one waiter accepts, the call disappears for the others.
  3. If nobody accepts within 30 seconds, the call goes to every tablet on shift; after 60 seconds to the manager and the bar screen with a signal; after 2 minutes to the manager on WhatsApp. The guest sees what is happening: "A waiter is coming" or "Passed to the manager".
  4. A screen at the bar: a tablet on charge, always on, loud. It shows every call and bill request with a timer. It does not depend on the waiters' tablets, so it is the main safety layer. The cashier works there too.
  5. Connection check: every tablet reports to the server once a minute. If one drops out for more than 2 minutes, the manager sees "Tablet 3 offline" and calls go to the others. A test call at the start of every shift: the waiter taps "Accept" and the system knows everything works.

If a tablet often lies on the counter, the same app can also go on a waiter's own phone as a second channel, if they want.

Tablet setup

Each tablet is set up once: autostart, no battery restrictions, notifications on the lock screen, full-screen calls, signal through "Do not disturb", volume. The app checks some of these settings itself and shows a red warning; the rest is covered by the test call at the start of the shift. Between shifts the tablets are on charge.

Payment

How the guest pays (option A)

  1. The guest taps "Bill" in the menu or asks a waiter.
  2. The waiter opens the table bill in Order Taker and enters the bill number and amount in our app on the same tablet.
  3. The tablet shows a QR code: QRIS for a banking app or a link to pay by card. The guest sees the table number and amount, checks them against the bill and pays.
  4. The payment provider sends our server a confirmation, and the server checks that it is genuine.
  5. The tablet and the bar screen show "Table 12 paid, Rp 412,000". The guest leaves only after that.
  6. The waiter marks the payment in Order Taker (or the cashier in PRESTO) as a separate method, "Online QR".
  7. At the end of the day the provider's payments are matched with the "Online QR" payments in the POS.

The bill can be split: several partial payments for one table, and the bill closes once the whole amount is paid. Order Taker already splits bills. Tips in the app can be added later if the owner wants.

Payment provider

  • Midtrans, DOKU and Xendit all fit: QRIS, Visa and Mastercard, e-wallets. The guest pays on the provider's page, and the provider sends a signed payment notification.
  • Payment is always in rupiah. The price in the guest's currency in the menu is only a guide, and a foreign card is converted by the guest's bank.
  • QRIS: under Bank Indonesia rules, since 1 October 2026 there is no fee up to Rp 100,000 and 0.7% above that. The fee cannot be passed on to the guest. The limit is Rp 10 million per payment. Midtrans's help page still shows 0.7% on everything (page from November 2025), so check when signing up.
  • Xendit adds Rp 4,000 to every payment, for cards even to a failed attempt, and from the second month charges at least 50 US dollars a month. On a Rp 400,000 bill paid by QRIS that is about Rp 6,800 against Rp 2,800 at Midtrans or DOKU, before VAT on the fee. Midtrans or DOKU suit a restaurant better.
  • Tourists from partner countries pay by QRIS with their home apps, and the restaurant receives rupiah at no extra cost. DOKU confirms Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, China and South Korea. According to Bank Indonesia most payment providers already support this; check with Midtrans and Xendit when signing up. Other guests pay by card, at about 2.9% + Rp 2,000.
  • Money does not arrive instantly. For QRIS, DOKU and Xendit pay out the next business day; check with Midtrans.
  • A QRIS refund is possible within 7 days and not with every app. Only the manager makes refunds.
  • The restaurant signs the contract in its own company's name, and the provider will ask for company documents. The money goes straight to the restaurant's account; we never hold it. Each location gets its own sales point with the provider, so payments and reconciliation stay separate.

Taxes and accounting

The amount to pay comes from the PRESTO bill, which already includes the restaurant tax and service charge. Every payment from the app must close a bill in the POS, otherwise revenue in the POS and at the provider will not match, and neither will tax reporting. That is what the daily reconciliation is for. The North Lombok tax office's rules for payments outside the POS should be checked with the restaurant's accountant.

If the internet goes down

The POS and Order Taker keep working over the restaurant network. Payment, ordering and calls in the app are unavailable, the menu asks the guest to call a waiter, and the guest pays as usual, in cash or at the card terminal.

Risks and protection

While the restaurant only has a menu, a mistake costs nothing. With payments every mistake costs money, so the aim is that any mistake is caught the same evening and costs no more than one bill.

Main rules

  1. Money counts as received only when the payment provider has confirmed it and the table has turned green on the waiter's tablet. The guest's screen, a screenshot or the guest's word do not count.
  2. The guest leaves after the green status or after paying another way.
  3. Every evening a reconciliation: provider payments against "Online QR" payments in PRESTO. Any mismatch is looked into the next day.
  4. First the provider's test mode and a check against the list of scenarios, then one location with a limit per payment, then the rest. We never tune the logic on the live system.
  5. A "switch off payment" button: one tap returns the menu to paying through the waiter, and the POS works as before.
  6. Card data never reaches us: the guest pays on the provider's page. The amount to pay is set by our server, not by the guest's phone.
  7. We do not collect guests' personal data (Indonesian personal data law).

Checks before launch

Each stage launches only when all its checks pass. We check in the payment provider's test mode, on the waiters' real tablets:

  • payment by QRIS and card, a bill split three ways, a failed payment, a cancellation;
  • payment when the connection drops, a double tap, an attempt to pay a bill that is already paid;
  • a full and a partial refund;
  • a fake table card during the morning check;
  • a call while the waiter is working in Order Taker; to a locked tablet; in silent mode; after the app is swiped away; after a restart; on a flat battery; on a tablet without internet;
  • nobody accepts a call: escalation up to the bar screen and WhatsApp;
  • switching payment off with one button;
  • an evening reconciliation with a deliberate error;
  • before stage 4 also: an order paid on the spot, re-entering an order in Order Taker and matching it;
  • before stage 5 also: an open tab with a hold, ordering beyond the hold, automatic closing after the guest leaves, a bank refusal at charging.

Where to start

  1. The owner writes to Probus (questions above), asks the accountant about taxes and makes the decisions in the next section.
  2. In parallel we build waiter calls on the tablets. This does not depend on Probus, as long as other apps can be installed on the tablets and they have internet (question 7).
  3. The restaurant signs a contract with a payment provider (Midtrans or DOKU). We test everything in test mode against the list above.
  4. We launch option A at one location with a limit per payment.
  5. Once Probus replies, we move to option B.

What the owner decides

  1. Whether the waiters' tablets are enough or their own phones are needed as a second channel for calls.
  2. When to move from one stage to the next.
  3. The limit per payment in the first weeks, and the table bill amount after which the waiter offers to settle.
  4. For stage 5: pay per order, an open tab on a card, or both for the guest to choose; the hold amount; after how many minutes without orders a bill closes by itself.
